How it works

Three steps. No blocklists.

A bond is a card authorization hold, not a charge. Nothing leaves a good sender's account.

1

A stranger reaches out

They use your link. Claude reads the message and prices the bond: about $1 for a genuine note, more for templated mass outreach.

2

They post a bond

A card hold via Stripe, or one API call for an AI agent. Only then does the message reach your inbox. Friends you approve skip this.

3

You decide in one click

Worth it or no action: the hold is released. Junk: the bond is captured and you keep it.

Built for agents

Access is an HTTP status.

An agent posts a message, gets a machine-readable 402 Payment Required, pays the hold and is delivered. No accounts, no humans in the loop. Spam agents pay for the pile; good agents get refunded.

FAQ

Good questions.

Is a bond a charge?

No. It is a card authorization hold. Funds are reserved, not taken. If you don't mark the message Junk, the hold is released automatically (within 72 hours).

What if I mark something Junk by mistake, or a sender disputes it?

Decisions are one-time, logged and shown with terms on the payment page. To prevent abuse, captures are capped per day and every inbox publishes its release rate, so senders can see how it behaves before paying.

Can I still hear from people I know?

Yes. Approved senders skip the bond. Unpaid or unverified mail waits 7 days in a waiting room. Nothing is deleted.

Does Claude control the money?

No. Claude only reads the message and suggests a price within a fixed range. Payments and releases are done by deterministic code and your button presses.

Is this real money?

Not yet. This is a hackathon prototype running on Stripe test mode. No real cards are charged.
