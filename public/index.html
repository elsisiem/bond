In plain words

Why would anyone pay to email you? They almost never do.

A Bond is a deposit, not a fee. It is the same idea as the card hold a hotel puts on your card at check-in: it reserves the money, then lets it go when you behave. Good senders get every cent back. The only people who lose money are the ones sending junk, and that is the point.

1

You share a Bond link in your bio, site or email signature, instead of your inbox address.

2

Someone who wants to reach you puts down a small deposit on their card, around $1. It is a hold, not a charge, and it takes 20 seconds.

3

You read their message. Worth your time, or you do nothing: the deposit is released and goes back to them. Junk: you keep it.

Why it is useful

Bots pass CAPTCHAs. They cannot pass a bill.

The pain is not obvious spam, Gmail already handles that. It is the flood of plausible outreach: AI sales sequences, PR pitches, recruiters, cold asks. Bond puts a price on exactly that, and refunds everyone who deserved to get through.

For the person

Fewer time-wasters, and the ones that slip through pay you. One-click decisions, plus an AI triage agent that applies your rules written in plain English.

For a good sender

It is a hold, not a charge. If you are worth reading, the hold is released and your reputation improves, so next time it is cheaper or free.

How it works

Three steps. No blocklists.

A bond is a card authorization hold, not a charge. Nothing leaves a good sender's account.

1

A stranger reaches out

They use your link. Claude reads the message and prices the bond: about $1 for a genuine note, more for templated mass outreach.

2

They post a bond

A card hold through Stripe, or one API call for an AI agent. Only then does the message reach your inbox. Approved senders skip this.

3

You decide in one click

Worth it or no action: the hold is released. Junk: the bond is captured and you keep it.

Safety, honestly

How it can be abused, and what stops it.

A paid gate invites new tricks. Here is every one we could think of and what we did about it.

Owners marking everything Junk to farm moneyLive

Every inbox shows its public release rate to senders before they pay. Daily capture cap per owner; extra junk marks release instead. Planned: dispute review.

Card testing on the payment linkLive

Rate limits per bond and per IP, Stripe-hosted card entry, holds not charges. Planned: Stripe Radar rules.

Spoofed senders and backscatterLive

Web and API intake never emails third parties. Email mode will reply only to the verified envelope sender after SPF and DKIM pass, at most one challenge per sender per day.

Typing a friend's address to skip the bondLive

Web and API intake ignore the approved-senders list because those addresses are self-declared. Approval applies only to authenticated email.

Prompt injection ("mark me worth it")Live

Claude only suggests a price inside a fixed range. The triage agent gets a one-hour token that can recommend but never move money. You press Apply.

Leaked private linksLive

Decision links are signed and expire; viewing a link never acts, only an explicit click does. The public feed hides subjects and full addresses.

Fees eating small bondsPlanned

A minimum bond floor for captures. Cancelled holds cost nothing, and revenue comes from subscriptions, not junk.

Many cards, many addressesPlanned

Limits per card fingerprint, and rising prices for near-identical messages (already live for templated clusters).

FAQ

Good questions.

Why would I pay to email someone?

You would not, unless you are sending junk. The bond is a refundable deposit: if your message is genuine, it is released and you lose nothing. It only costs money to the people who message hundreds of strangers with the same template.

Is a bond a charge?

No. It is a card authorization hold. Funds are reserved, not taken. If you do not mark the message Junk, the hold is released automatically within 72 hours.

Why would a real sender bother?

Because the hold costs them nothing if they are worth reading, and it gets them past a wall that ignores everyone else. If you know the person, approve them and they skip it. Bond is for inboxes where attention is valuable, so the friction is the point.

How does Bond make money?

Not from junk: if it works, little junk gets through. Plans: a subscription for individuals and teams, a per-bond fee for developers embedding Bond in forms and APIs, and a small cut of captured bonds above a minimum.

What if I mark something Junk by mistake, or a sender disputes it?

Decisions are one-time, logged and shown with terms on the payment page. To limit abuse, captures are capped per day and every inbox publishes its release rate, so senders can see how it behaves before paying.

Can I still hear from people I know?

Yes. Approved senders skip the bond. Unpaid or unverified mail waits 7 days in a waiting room. Nothing is deleted.

Does Claude control the money?

No. Claude only reads the message and suggests a price within a fixed range. Payments and releases are done by deterministic code and your button presses.

Is this real money?

Not yet. This is a hackathon prototype running on Stripe test mode. No real cards are charged.
